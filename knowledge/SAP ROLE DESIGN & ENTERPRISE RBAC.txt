Q1. What is Role Design?
Interviewer's Objective
The interviewer wants to understand whether you know how to build an enterprise
authorization model—not just maintain existing roles.
Executive Answer
Role Design is the process of translating business responsibilities into secure,
maintainable, and auditable SAP authorization roles.
A well-designed role should:
Grant only the access required to perform assigned business activities.
Enforce the Principle of Least Privilege.
Prevent Segregation of Duties (SoD) conflicts.
Be reusable across multiple users.
Be scalable across business units and countries.
Minimize long-term maintenance effort.
Role design is not a technical exercise performed only in PFCG. It begins with
understanding business processes, organizational structures, and governance
requirements.

Q8. What is Role Explosion?
A favorite architecture interview question.
Executive Answer
Role Explosion occurs when organizations create too many narrowly defined or user-
specific roles instead of reusable business roles.
Example
A company has:
5 countries
10 departments
20 job functions
Poor design may result in:
5 × 10 × 20 = 1,000+ unique roles

Many of these roles differ only by minor organizational values.
Consequences
High maintenance
Increased testing effort
Complex audits
Difficult user provisioning
Prevention
Master/Derived Role Strategy
Standardized RBAC
Business Role Catalog
Organizational restrictions
Regular role rationalization
Q9. What is Role Rationalization?
Executive Answer
Role Rationalization is the process of reviewing the existing role landscape to eliminate
redundancy and improve governance.
Typical activities include:
Remove duplicate roles
Merge similar roles
Retire obsolete roles
Standardize naming
Reduce authorization overlap
Eliminate unused transactions
Benefits
Simpler administration
Improved audit readiness
Reduced maintenance
Better user experience

Q10. Describe your role design approach during an
S/4HANA transformation.
This question aligns closely with your uploaded job description.
Executive Answer
During an S/4HANA transformation, I avoid simply converting ECC roles. Instead, I
redesign the authorization model to align with modern SAP capabilities.
My approach includes:
1. Review existing ECC roles and identify obsolete transactions.
2. Conduct business workshops to validate future-state processes.
3. Define business personas and an enterprise RBAC model.
4. Build new business and technical roles rather than copying legacy roles.
5. Design Fiori catalogs, spaces, and pages.
6. Validate roles using SAP GRC ARA for SoD compliance.
7. Execute comprehensive security testing during SIT and UAT.
8. Support production cutover and hypercare.
9. Perform post-go-live optimization based on usage and feedback.
This approach reduces technical debt, improves governance, and creates a security model
aligned with S/4HANA best practices rather than carrying forward historical design issues.
Architect's Insight
One of the biggest mistakes I see candidates make is describing role design as a PFCG
activity. At senior levels, role design starts long before PFCG. It begins with understanding
business processes, defining personas, assessing risks, and designing a governance
model. PFCG is simply the implementation tool.
