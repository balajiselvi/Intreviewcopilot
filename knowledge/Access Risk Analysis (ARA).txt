

CATEGORY 4 – SAP GRC ACCESS CONTROL 
Chapter 2 – Access Risk Analysis (ARA) 
 
Q11. What is Access Risk Analysis (ARA)? 
Interviewer's Objective 
The interviewer wants to determine whether you understand ARA as a risk governance 
solution, not merely a report. 
 
Executive Answer 
Access Risk Analysis (ARA) is the SAP GRC module responsible for identifying, 
analyzing, and managing authorization risks across SAP landscapes. 
It evaluates users, roles, profiles, and HR objects against predefined business risk rules 
to detect: 
• Segregation of Duties (SoD) conflicts  
• Critical Access  
• Critical Permissions  
• Sensitive Transactions  
• Custom Risk Rules  
ARA enables organizations to proactively identify security risks before access is 
provisioned and continuously monitor existing access throughout the user lifecycle. 
 
Business Objective 
ARA helps answer one simple question: 
"Does this access introduce unacceptable business risk?" 
 
Q12. What types of Risk Analysis can be performed in ARA? 
Executive Answer 
SAP GRC supports multiple types of analysis. 
1. User Level Risk Analysis 

Checks risks assigned to a specific user. 
Example 
User 
 
↓ 
 
BALAJI 
 
↓ 
 
Roles 
 
↓ 
 
Risk Analysis 
 
↓ 
 
High Risk Found 
 
2. Role Risk Analysis 
Checks whether a role itself contains SoD conflicts. 
Useful during: 
• Role Design  
• Role Modification  
• S/4HANA Migration  
 
3. Profile Analysis 
Checks authorization profiles instead of roles. 
Less commonly used. 
 
4. HR Object Analysis 
Analyzes risks assigned through HR organizational structures. 

 
5. Organizational Rule Analysis 
Evaluates SoD considering: 
• Company Code  
• Plant  
• Purchasing Organization  
• Sales Organization  
This significantly reduces false positives.

Interview Tip 
During an S/4HANA implementation, perform Role Risk Analysis before assigning 
roles to users. This prevents SoD issues from being introduced into production.

Q22. How do you reduce false positives in ARA? 
One of the most practical interview questions. 
 
Executive Answer 
False positives occur when GRC reports a theoretical conflict that is not possible in 
practice. 
I reduce them by: 
• Maintaining organizational rules (Company Code, Plant, Purchasing 
Organization, etc.)  
• Removing obsolete transactions from the rule set.  
• Validating custom transactions and Fiori apps.  
• Eliminating duplicate actions.  
• Reviewing role design to ensure least privilege.  
• Periodically cleansing unused roles and authorizations.  
• Updating the rule set after business process changes.  
The goal is to ensure that ARA highlights genuine business risks rather than 
overwhelming reviewers with unnecessary findings. 
 
Q23. ARA Performance Tuning 
Interviewer 
"Risk Analysis is taking several hours. How would you troubleshoot it?" 
Executive Answer 
I would investigate the issue systematically: 
1. Verify the scope of the analysis (users, roles, systems).  
2. Check the health and consistency of the rule set.  
3. Review connector performance and RFC connectivity.  

4. Analyze background job execution and scheduling.  
5. Ensure indexes and database statistics are current.  
6. Remove duplicate or obsolete actions from the rule set.  
7. Validate that unnecessary organizational levels are not increasing processing 
complexity.  
8. Coordinate with Basis to review system resources if required.  
Performance tuning should preserve accuracy while improving execution time. 
 
Real Interview Scenario 
Interviewer 
"A business user's access request fails because ARA identifies a High SoD risk. The 
business insists the access is essential. What would you do?" 
Executive Answer 
I would not bypass the risk simply because the business requests it. 
My approach would be: 
1. Review the ARA findings to confirm the conflict.  
2. Validate whether the requested access is genuinely required for the user's 
responsibilities.  
3. Check if the conflict can be resolved through role redesign or by removing 
unnecessary access.  
4. If separation is not operationally feasible, discuss compensating controls with 
the business and Internal Audit.  
5. If the organization accepts the residual risk, document it through an approved 
mitigation control in SAP GRC and obtain the necessary approvals.  
6. Schedule periodic reviews to ensure the mitigation remains appropriate.  
This approach balances operational requirements with governance and ensures the 
decision is transparent, documented, and auditable. 
 
Senior Consultant Interview Tips 
• Explain business impact, not just configuration.  

• Emphasize that ARA should be used during role design, not only after users are 
provisioned.  
• Highlight collaboration with business owners, auditors, and compliance teams 
when defining rule sets and risk acceptance.  
• Distinguish between theoretical SoD conflicts and actual business risk by 
discussing organizational-level analysis and compensating controls.
