# Self-Introduction & Professional Narrative

## Overview

Self-introduction is often the first question in an SAP interview: "Tell me about yourself." or "Walk me through your resume." This question sets tone for entire interview and gives candidate 2-3 minutes to establish credibility, relevance, and interest. Strong self-introductions: structured (career arc clear), relevant (highlight SAP experience, not everything), concise (2-3 minutes), and confident (natural speaking, not memorized). Self-introduction is not life story or CV recitation; it's curated narrative positioning candidate as right fit for this specific role. Weak introductions: ramble, irrelevant details, unclear career path, sound scripted.

## Interview Summary

Self-introduction: curated 2-3 minute narrative covering: background, career progression (why each move?), SAP experience (what projects?), current focus, why this role. Structure: past (where from?), present (what doing?), future (where going?). Avoid: life story, CV recitation, rambling, irrelevant details. Strong: clear, confident, relevant, positions you as right fit.

## 30 Second Interview Answer

Self-intro structure (2-3 minutes): (1) **Opening:** "I'm [Name], I have [X years] SAP experience in [domain]." (2) **Career arc:** "Started as [role], moved to [role], now [current role]." (3) **SAP experience:** "Led [project], technologies [SAP products], outcomes [business impact]." (4) **Current focus:** "Focused on [area], interested in [growth area]." (5) **Why this role:** "Attracted to this opportunity because [reason relevant to job]."

Illustrative approach: fill this structure only from candidate background — name, years, domains, roles, and outcomes that are actually documented. If background is missing, describe the structure you would use rather than inventing employers, headcount, or percentages.

## 60 Second Interview Answer

**Self-introduction pattern (use only facts from candidate background):**

Opening: years and domain that are documented. Career arc: roles actually held. SAP scope: products and programs listed in the resume. Current focus and why this role: mapped to the job description, not invented company research.

If candidate background is absent, say how you would structure the intro. Do not invent employers, revenue, user counts, uptime, or savings.

## 90 Second Interview Answer

Same five-part structure with more room: credentials, why each career move, two documented SAP scopes, current focus, why this role. Keep numbers only when they appear in candidate background. A Principal IAM interview intro should foreground authorization design, identity lifecycle, GRC/IAG, and delivery judgment — not a fictional program biography.

## Architecture

- Self-intro structure: (1) Opening (credentials), (2) Career arc (why each move?), (3) SAP experience (projects, technologies), (4) Current focus, (5) Why this role
- 2-3 minutes (not 30 seconds, not 10 minutes)
- Curated (not life story, not CV recitation)
- Relevant (positions you for this specific role)
- Confident (natural, not scripted)

## Runtime Flow

1. Listen to question ("Tell me about yourself")
2. Start with credentials (name, years, domain)
3. Walk through career arc (why each move?)
4. Highlight SAP projects and outcomes
5. Describe current focus and interests
6. Connect to this opportunity (why you're interested)
7. Invite follow-up ("Any questions about my background?")

## Configuration

- Career narrative (progression is logical, clear)
- SAP projects (specific, relevant to role)
- Technologies (aligned with job description)
- Outcomes (business impact, not just activities)
- Positioning (why you're right fit for this role)

## Implementation Activities

- Develop career narrative (why each move? how does it connect?)
- Identify 3-5 key SAP projects (most relevant to target role)
- Practice delivery (natural, not scripted)
- Prepare for follow-ups (deep questions on specific projects)
- Tailor for each role (emphasize relevant experience)

## Production Interview Activities

- Deliver smoothly (confident, engaging)
- Read interviewer cues (adjust depth if needed)
- Connect to interviewer's needs (show you understand the role)
- Prepare for follow-up questions

## Troubleshooting

**Common issue:** Introduction too long (5+ minutes, interviewer bored).
Root cause: Not practiced, or trying to cover everything
Resolution: Practice 2-3 minute version. Cut non-essential details. Focus on career arc + key projects + why this role.

**Common issue:** Introduction sounds scripted (memorized, robotic).
Root cause: Over-prepared, reading from notes
Resolution: Practice until natural (not memorized). Talk like you're explaining to colleague, not reading script.

**Common issue:** Introduction irrelevant to role (talks about skills they didn't ask for).
Root cause: Generic introduction, not tailored to job
Resolution: Tailor introduction (emphasize experience relevant to this role). "I'm excited about this role because it combines my [relevant experience] with your [company need]."

**Common issue:** Introduction vague (unclear career progression or projects).
Root cause: Not clearly thought through, or nervous
Resolution: Practice specific details (project names, company sizes, timelines, outcomes). Specificity builds credibility.

## Common Interview Questions

1. **Tell me about yourself.** (covered above)
2. **Walk me through your resume.** (same structure as introduction)
3. **What attracted you to this role?** (covered in introduction's "Why this opportunity" section)
4. **What do you see yourself doing in 5 years?** (future direction, aligned with company's vision)
5. **Why are you leaving your current role?** (career growth, new challenges, company strategy change)

## Tough Follow-up Questions

1. **You've had 5 different jobs in 8 years. Why so much movement?**
   - Explain each move (was it growth-driven, or running away from bad situation?).
   - Recommendation: show progression (each role more senior, more strategic). "I moved to new roles for growth opportunities, not running from problems."
   - If true job-hopping: be honest ("I was exploring different areas to find right fit").

2. **You say you led a 50-person team. But your title is 'Architect,' not 'Manager.' How did you lead without authority?**
   - Explain: architectural leadership (make decisions) vs people management (hire/fire).
   - "I led through influence (expertise, relationships), not authority. People followed because I made good decisions, not because I was their boss."

3. **Your last company is struggling. Does that reflect on your work there?**
   - Separate personal accomplishment from company performance.
   - "My work was successful (projects on-time, team performed well). Company's struggles were market-driven (industry changes), not execution issues."

## Best Practices

- Develop clear career narrative (progression is logical)
- Practice delivery (natural, confident, 2-3 minutes)
- Tailor for each role (emphasize relevant experience)
- Include specific projects and outcomes (credibility)
- Connect to company's needs (show you understand the role)
- Prepare for follow-ups (be ready to go deeper)

## Common Mistakes

- Too long (5+ minutes, loses interviewer)
- Too scripted (memorized, robotic)
- Irrelevant details (life story, not career narrative)
- Vague on specifics (projects, outcomes, technologies)
- Not tailored (generic introduction for every job)
- Negative framing ("I was stuck in my last role, needed to leave")

## Interviewer's Hidden Expectations

Strong candidates: (1) **Clear narrative** (career arc makes sense), (2) **Relevant experience** (aligned with role), (3) **Specific details** (credibility), (4) **Confident delivery** (natural, engaging), (5) **Understanding of role** (connect your experience to company's needs).

Weak candidates: Rambling, scripted, vague, irrelevant, defensive.

## What Makes This a 10/10 Answer

- Clear career progression (logical moves)
- Specific SAP projects (names, companies, outcomes)
- Relevant to this role (shows you understand the job)
- Confident delivery (natural, engaging, 2-3 minutes)
- Connects to company (shows you've researched, understand needs)
- Professional narrative (tailored, not generic)

## Red Flags

- Rambling (no clear structure)
- Scripted (memorized, sounds robotic)
- Irrelevant (talks about non-SAP experience for SAP role)
- Vague (no specific projects or outcomes)
- Negative framing ("I had to leave my last job")
- Job-hopping pattern (no clear progression)
