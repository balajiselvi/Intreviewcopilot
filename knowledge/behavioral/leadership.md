# Leadership Style & Team Development

## Overview

Leadership in SAP context: guiding teams through complex implementations, making architectural decisions, mentoring junior staff, managing stakeholder expectations, creating psychological safety. Interview questions test: leadership philosophy (command-and-control vs collaborative?), decision-making approach (data-driven vs intuitive?), team development (do you invest in junior staff?), accountability (own mistakes?), vision (where are you taking the team?). Strong leaders: collaborative (empower team), decisive (make calls when needed), accountable (own decisions), invest in people (mentoring, development), clear vision. Weak leaders: micromanage, indecisive, blame others, ignore development, no vision.

## 30 Second Interview Answer

Leadership approach: (1) **Collaborative** (empower team, don't micromanage). (2) **Decisive** (make calls when needed). (3) **Accountable** (own decisions, own mistakes). (4) **Develop people** (mentoring, growth). (5) **Clear vision** (where are we going?).

Example: "I lead collaboratively—empower team to make decisions. But I'm decisive when needed (architectural choices, priorities). I own my decisions (good and bad). I invest in junior staff (mentoring, training, growth). I communicate clear vision (what are we building, why does it matter?). Result: team is engaged, decisions are sound, junior staff grows into senior roles."

## 60 Second Interview Answer

**Leadership Example: Guiding Implementation Team**

**Situation:**
Leading 50-person S/4HANA implementation team. Multiple domains (Finance, Supply Chain, HR), complex stakeholder landscape.

**Leadership Approach:**

1. **Collaborative (empower team):**
   - "I don't micromanage. Each team lead owns their domain. I set direction, they execute."
   - Weekly team meetings: each domain reports progress, blockers, decisions
   - Empower team to make decisions (quality, testing approach, go-live strategy)

2. **Decisive (when needed):**
   - Architecture decisions: I make calls (cloud vs on-premise, data model, integration approach)
   - Escalations: if team stuck, I unblock (negotiate with stakeholders, adjust scope/timeline)
   - When consensus hard to find: I decide (explain reasoning, move forward)

3. **Accountable:**
   - "I own the program. Success and failure are my responsibility."
   - When project behind: I report honestly to steering committee (not blame team)
   - When decisions go wrong: I own it (learn lesson, prevent recurrence)

4. **Develop people:**
   - Junior architects: pair them with seniors (knowledge transfer)
   - Team leads: give them stretch assignments (grow their capability)
   - Mentoring: 1-on-1 monthly with direct reports (career development)
   - Promotion: when junior architect ready, I advocate for them

5. **Clear vision:**
   - "Here's what we're building: modern S/4HANA system, quarterly updates ready, Clean Core discipline."
   - "Why it matters: business agility, lower cost, faster innovation."
   - "How we get there: phased rollout, strong governance, quality discipline."
   - Regular all-hands (celebrate progress, remind vision, address concerns)

**Result:**
- Team engaged and proud of work
- On-time delivery (most projects are)
- Low turnover (team stays because they're growing)
- Junior staff developed (5 junior architects promoted to senior in past 3 years)

## 90 Second Interview Answer

**Leadership Philosophy: Collaborative + Decisive**

**Core Philosophy:**
I believe leadership is about enabling your team to do their best work, while making clear decisions when needed. Not about "being in charge," but about setting direction and removing obstacles.

**In Practice:**

**Collaborative Leadership:**
- Empower team: architects make architectural decisions (within guardrails), developers own code quality
- Psychological safety: people feel safe to speak up (disagreement is welcome, bad news surfaced early)
- Listening: I ask questions before giving answers (understand perspective)
- Distributed decision-making: decisions made closest to work (not everything escalated to me)

**Decisive When Needed:**
- Architecture: I make calls (cloud vs on-prem, data model, integration approach)
- Blockers: if team stuck, I escalate/negotiate (remove obstacles)
- When consensus impossible: I decide (transparent reasoning, move forward)
- Trade-off calls: scope vs timeline vs quality—I decide based on business priorities

**Accountability:**
- "Buck stops with me" (success and failure are my responsibility)
- Own decisions (even when go wrong)
- Learn from mistakes (prevent recurrence, don't blame team)
- Honest reporting (tell steering committee what's really happening)

**Develop People:**
- Mentoring: invest time in junior staff (1-on-1, career conversations)
- Stretch assignments: give junior staff challenging work (grow capability, not just execute)
- Knowledge transfer: pair junior with senior (learn from experience)
- Promotion: when ready, advocate for advancement
- Learning culture: team knows growth is valued

**Clear Vision:**
- "Here's what we're building and why it matters" (connect work to business purpose)
- "Here's how we get there" (roadmap, milestones)
- "Here's what excellence looks like" (quality standards, expectations)
- Regular communication: reinforce vision (all-hands, team meetings)

**Example—Difficult Situation (Project Behind Timeline):**

Situation: Finance implementation 3 weeks behind schedule. Steering committee pressuring go-live date.

Leadership response:
1. **Honest assessment:** "We're behind because [reasons]. Going live on schedule would mean cutting testing (risky). Here are options: (A) Slip 3 weeks (finish properly), (B) Reduce scope (go live without non-critical features), (C) Risk it (cut testing, hope for best)."

2. **Empower team:** Ask implementation lead: "What do you recommend? What would you need to catch up?"

3. **Decisive:** If team can catch up with resources, approve resources. If can't catch up, recommend slip (not C).

4. **Accountable:** "This is my call. I'm recommitting to schedule if we reduce scope, or recommending slip if we keep scope. Both options are acceptable to me. Steering committee decides."

5. **Develop team:** Debrief after crisis: "What did we learn? How do we prevent this next time?" Coach team lead on estimation/planning.

Result: Steering committee makes informed decision. Team feels supported (not blamed). Team learns for next project.

## Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Micromanages (team not engaged)
Resolution: Trust team, empower decisions, stay informed but not involved

**Issue:** Indecisive (team frustrated by lack of direction)
Resolution: Make calls when needed, explain reasoning, move forward

**Issue:** Blames team (lacks accountability)
Resolution: Own decisions, own mistakes, "buck stops with me"

**Issue:** Ignores development (junior staff leave)
Resolution: Invest time in mentoring, stretch assignments, career conversations

## Tough Follow-up Questions

1. **You say "empower team." But what if team makes bad decision?**
   - Depends on severity. Minor mistake: let them learn. Major mistake: intervene, coach (not punish).

2. **You take accountability for team's work. Don't they need to be accountable?**
   - Yes, both. I'm accountable for overall program. Team members accountable for their work. Both.

3. **You mentor junior staff. Doesn't that take time away from your own work?**
   - Yes, but ROI is high (junior staff grow, eventually don't need mentoring, become leaders). Worth it.

4. **You say "clear vision." What if business keeps changing strategy?**
   - Adapt vision as needed, but communicate clearly (why changed, what's new direction). Keep team informed.
