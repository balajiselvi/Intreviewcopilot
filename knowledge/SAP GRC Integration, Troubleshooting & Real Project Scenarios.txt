Chapter 8 – SAP GRC Integration, Troubleshooting & Real Project Scenarios 
 
Q98. Explain the SAP GRC Landscape Architecture. 
Interviewer's Objective 
The interviewer wants to understand whether you know how SAP GRC integrates 
with enterprise SAP landscapes. 
 
Executive Answer 
SAP GRC Access Control is typically deployed as a centralized governance system 
that connects to multiple SAP and non-SAP systems through connectors. 
A typical enterprise landscape includes: 
                     SAP GRC AC 
                          │ 
 ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐ 
 │          │          │          │ 
ECC      S/4HANA      BW      SuccessFactors 
 │          │          │          │ 
 └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘ 
            │ 
       SAP IDM / IAS / IPS 
            │ 
      Microsoft Entra ID 
SAP GRC acts as the governance layer, while target systems enforce the 
authorizations. 
 
Q99. Explain how SAP GRC integrates with S/4HANA. 
Executive Answer 
During S/4HANA implementations, SAP GRC integrates with: 
• Business Roles  
• Fiori Applications  
• Backend Authorizations  

• Embedded Analytics  
• CDS Views  
• OData Services  
The GRC rule set must be updated to include: 
• New S/4HANA transactions  
• Fiori applications  
• Business Partner transactions  
• Embedded business functions  
Simply migrating ECC rule sets without updates leads to inaccurate risk analysis. 
 
Q100. How does SAP GRC integrate with SAP IDM? 
 
Executive Answer 
SAP IDM manages the identity lifecycle, while SAP GRC governs access decisions. 
SAP IDM Responsibilities 
• Joiner  
• Mover  
• Leaver  
• User creation  
• Identity synchronization  
• HR integration  
SAP GRC Responsibilities 
• Risk analysis  
• SoD validation  
• Approval workflow  
• Emergency access  
• Role governance  
Together, they provide end-to-end identity governance. 

 
Q101. Explain SAP GRC Integration with IAS and IPS. 
 
Executive Answer 
In cloud-first landscapes: 
SAP Identity Authentication Services (IAS) 
• Authentication  
• Single Sign-On  
• Trust relationships  
• Multi-factor authentication  
SAP Identity Provisioning Services (IPS) 
• User provisioning  
• Identity synchronization  
• Cloud application integration  
SAP GRC complements IAS and IPS by governing access requests and ensuring 
provisioning follows approved workflows and risk analysis. 
 
Q102. Explain GRC Connector Architecture. 
 
Executive Answer 
A connector enables communication between SAP GRC and a target system. 
Through the connector, SAP GRC can: 
• Retrieve users  
• Retrieve roles  
• Execute provisioning  
• Perform risk analysis  
• Synchronize data  
Typical connector technologies include RFC for on-premise SAP systems and 
supported integration mechanisms for cloud solutions. 

 
Q103. Production Issue: ARA Returns No Risks 
Interviewer 
"ARA suddenly reports no risks for any user. What would you check?" 
 
Executive Answer 
My investigation would be systematic: 
Step 1 
Verify connector status. 
 
Step 2 
Confirm synchronization jobs completed successfully. 
 
Step 3 
Validate the active rule set. 
 
Step 4 
Check whether risk analysis is pointing to the correct rule set. 
 
Step 5 
Verify authorization synchronization. 
 
Step 6 
Review background job execution. 
 
Step 7 
Review application logs. 
 

Step 8 
Validate recent transports. 
 
The objective is to determine whether the issue lies with data synchronization, 
configuration, or the rule set itself. 
 
Q104. Production Issue: ARM Request Not Provisioned 
 
Executive Answer 
My troubleshooting approach: 
1. Check request status.  
2. Review MSMP stage.  
3. Validate BRF+ routing.  
4. Verify approvals.  
5. Check connector communication.  
6. Review provisioning logs.  
7. Verify target system availability.  
8. Confirm user lock status.  
9. Validate provisioning authorization.  
10. Retry after root cause correction.  
 
Q105. Firefighter Logs Not Generated 
 
Executive Answer 
Possible causes include: 
• Logging configuration issue  
• Connector communication failure  
• Incorrect Firefighter configuration  

• Authorization issues  
• Background jobs not running  
• Log synchronization failure  
I would validate each component systematically before implementing corrective 
actions. 
 
Q106. Business Reports Excessive SoD Violations 
 
Executive Answer 
My approach would be: 
• Review organizational rules.  
• Eliminate false positives.  
• Remove obsolete transactions.  
• Review duplicate functions.  
• Validate custom transactions.  
• Prioritize high-risk conflicts.  
• Update the rule set where appropriate.  
The goal is to improve the quality of risk reporting rather than simply reducing the 
number of findings. 
 
Q107. During Go-Live, Hundreds of Access Requests Fail 
 
Executive Answer 
I would: 
1. Identify whether the issue is workflow, connector, or provisioning related.  
2. Prioritize business-critical users.  
3. Establish a temporary command center with Security, Basis, Functional, and 
Business representatives.  
4. Use approved emergency access procedures where justified.  

5. Monitor requests until stability is restored.  
6. Perform a post-go-live review to identify root causes and implement 
preventive improvements.  
 
Q108. SAP GRC Upgrade Strategy 
 
Executive Answer 
Before upgrading SAP GRC: 
• Review SAP Notes.  
• Assess compatibility with connected systems.  
• Back up configuration.  
• Validate connectors.  
• Test rule sets.  
• Test workflows.  
• Execute regression testing.  
• Validate emergency access.  
• Confirm provisioning.  
• Perform production readiness review.  
Never upgrade GRC without end-to-end testing because governance failures affect 
every connected SAP system.

Advanced Interview Scenario 1 
Interviewer 
"The business wants all SoD violations cleared before go-live. Is that realistic?" 
 
Executive Answer 
Not necessarily. 
The objective should be to eliminate unacceptable business risk, not simply 
achieve zero reported violations. 
Some conflicts may be operationally necessary and can be managed through 
approved mitigation controls, provided they are documented, monitored, and 
periodically reviewed. 
The focus should be on: 
• Eliminating unnecessary conflicts.  
• Redesigning roles where practical.  
• Applying compensating controls where justified.  
• Ensuring governance decisions are approved by the business.  
 
Advanced Interview Scenario 2 
Interviewer 
"The CIO asks you: 'Why should we invest in SAP GRC when we already have SAP 
Security?'" 

 
Executive Answer 
SAP Security and SAP GRC serve complementary purposes. 
SAP Security determines how access is technically implemented. 
SAP GRC determines whether that access should be granted, whether it introduces 
unacceptable business risk, who approves it, how emergency access is controlled, 
and how compliance is demonstrated. 
Without SAP GRC, organizations often rely on manual approvals, spreadsheets, 
and periodic reviews, making governance inconsistent and audits more difficult. 
SAP GRC introduces automation, traceability, risk management, and policy 
enforcement across the access lifecycle. 
 
Advanced Interview Scenario 3 (Manufacturing) 
Interviewer 
"A plant is down because operators cannot post goods movements after a role 
redesign. Production is losing millions every hour. What do you do?" 
 
Executive Answer 
I would follow an incident management approach: 
1. Confirm the business impact and affected transactions.  
2. Assemble the response team (Security, Basis, Functional, Production).  
3. Use SU53 and STAUTHTRACE to identify authorization failures.  
4. Compare the new role with the previous production role.  
5. Determine whether the issue is due to missing authorization objects, 
organizational values, or Fiori configuration.  
6. If immediate restoration is required, use approved Emergency Access 
procedures while documenting all activities.  
7. Implement the permanent role correction through the standard transport 
process.  
8. Conduct a root cause analysis and update role design standards to prevent 
recurrence.  

This approach balances rapid recovery with governance. 
 
Advanced Interview Scenario 4 (Executive) 
Interviewer 
"If you joined us tomorrow, what would be the first five things you would assess in 
our SAP Security and GRC environment?" 
 
Executive Answer 
My initial assessment would focus on: 
1. Role Landscape – Role count, duplication, RBAC maturity, and naming 
standards.  
2. SoD & Critical Access – High-risk conflicts, mitigation quality, and rule set 
effectiveness.  
3. Access Governance – ARM workflows, approvals, and provisioning 
performance.  
4. Emergency Access – Firefighter usage, log reviews, owner/controller 
assignments, and overdue reviews.  
5. Audit Readiness – Role ownership, periodic access reviews, documentation, 
and open audit findings.  
These areas provide a quick view of both operational effectiveness and governance 
maturity.
