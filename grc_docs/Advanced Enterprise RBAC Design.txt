Q11. Explain an Enterprise RBAC Framework.
Interviewer's Objective
The interviewer wants to know whether you can design SAP Security for a global
organization rather than maintaining individual roles.
Executive Answer
Enterprise Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is a governance model that standardizes
authorization management across the organization by aligning access with business
responsibilities instead of individual users.
The objective is to create reusable, scalable, and auditable roles that support business
operations while enforcing security policies and compliance requirements.
The framework consists of the following layers:

Q15. How do you prevent Role Explosion?
One of the most frequently asked Lead-level questions.
Executive Answer
Role Explosion occurs when organizations create unnecessary or user-specific roles.
I prevent this by:
Standardized Personas
One role for one business function.

Q16. Explain Segregation of Duties (SoD) from a Role
Design perspective.
Executive Answer

SoD is not merely a GRC report.
It should be considered during role design.
Example
A single role should never contain:
Vendor Creation
AND
Vendor Payment
because one user could create a fraudulent vendor and immediately release payment.
Preventive Approach
Identify risks before building roles.
Run SAP GRC ARA during the design phase.
Modify roles before production.
Detective Approach
Periodic SoD reviews.
Corrective Approach
Role redesign.
Mitigation controls.
Q17. How do you design roles for Fiori?
Executive Answer
Unlike SAP GUI, Fiori requires two security layers.

Frontend Role
Contains:
Catalogs
Spaces
Pages
Target Mapping
Responsible for:
"What users see."
Backend Role
Contains:
Authorization Objects
Organizational Values
Business Transactions
Responsible for:
"What users can do."
Best Practice
Never combine frontend and backend roles.
Maintain them independently.
This simplifies maintenance and future upgrades.

Q20. Describe the biggest role redesign project you
have worked on.

This is an excellent behavioral and technical question.
Executive Answer (Tailored to Your Background)
Interview Case Study
Interviewer
"You have inherited an SAP system with 8,000 users, 4,500 roles, and no documentation.
How would you approach the redesign?"
Executive Answer
I would take a structured, phased approach:
1. Discovery
Inventory all roles, users, and usage statistics.
Identify duplicate, obsolete, and inactive roles.
2. Business Analysis
Conduct workshops with process owners.
One of the largest projects I supported involved an enterprise S/4HANA transformation
across a global manufacturing organization. My responsibility was to lead the SAP
Security workstream, including RBAC redesign, Fiori security architecture, SAP GRC
governance, and security cutover planning.
We began by analyzing the existing ECC role landscape and found a significant
number of redundant and user-specific roles. Working with business process owners
and functional consultants, we defined standardized business personas and developed
a master/derived role strategy aligned with global business processes.
We incorporated SAP GRC Access Risk Analysis throughout the design phase to
identify and eliminate SoD conflicts before deployment. We also redesigned Fiori
security using separate frontend and backend roles, enabling better governance and
easier maintenance.
During testing, we supported SIT, UAT, and cutover activities, ensuring users received
the correct access at go-live. Post-implementation, we monitored access issues during
hypercare and optimized roles based on actual business usage.
The outcome was a simplified role landscape, improved audit readiness, stronger
governance, and a security model aligned with S/4HANA best practices while
supporting business operations across multiple regions.

Architect's Insight
A senior consultant is expected to build roles. An architect is expected to design a
sustainable authorization strategy that can support thousands of users, multiple countries,
evolving business processes, and future S/4HANA enhancements without constant
redesign.
