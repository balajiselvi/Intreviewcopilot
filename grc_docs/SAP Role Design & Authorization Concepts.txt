Q11. What is Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)?
Interviewer's Objective
The interviewer wants to understand whether you know how to design enterprise roles—
not just create them in PFCG.
Executive Answer
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is a security model where access is assigned to
business roles instead of directly assigning authorizations to individual users.
Each role represents a specific job function within the organization, ensuring users receive
only the access required to perform their responsibilities.
Rather than managing thousands of individual authorizations, organizations manage roles
that are aligned with business processes.

Never design roles around people.
Always design roles around business responsibilities.

Interview Follow-up
Do you modify SU24?
Answer
Yes, but only after careful review.
For custom transactions or Z programs, we validate the authorization logic and update
SU24 proposals to ensure future role generation remains consistent.
Changes should follow governance processes because incorrect SU24 settings affect
every future role built from that transaction.
Q16. What is User Comparison?
Executive Answer

After assigning a role to a user, SAP does not immediately update the user's authorization
buffer.
User Comparison synchronizes the assigned roles and generated profiles with the user
master record.
Without User Comparison,
Users may not receive newly assigned authorizations until the next synchronization.

Q18. Explain RFC Security.
This is a favorite question in architecture interviews.
Executive Answer
Remote Function Calls (RFCs) enable communication between SAP systems or between
SAP and external applications.
RFC security focuses on ensuring these communications are secure and use only the
minimum required privileges.
Best Practices
✔ Never use Dialog users for RFCs.
✔ Use Communication or System users.
✔ Restrict S_RFC authorization objects.
✔ Secure RFC destinations (SM59).
✔ Use SNC encryption where applicable.
✔ Monitor trusted RFC connections regularly.
Interview Question
What risks arise from using SAP_ALL on an RFC user?
Answer
Granting SAP_ALL to an RFC user creates a high-risk attack vector. If the credentials are
compromised, an attacker may gain unrestricted access to the target system, bypassing the
principle of least privilege and significantly increasing compliance and security risks.

Q20. Explain the Principle of Least Privilege in a Real
Project.
Executive Answer
In one S/4HANA transformation, business users requested broad access to speed up
testing. Instead of granting SAP_ALL, we designed temporary project roles limited to the
required transactions and organizational values.
Access requests were approved through SAP GRC ARM, reviewed for SoD conflicts using
ARA, and removed after testing. This approach supported the project while maintaining
compliance and avoiding unnecessary security exposure.
Advanced Interview Scenario

Interviewer
A Finance Director tells you, "My team is losing productivity because security is too
restrictive. Give them SAP_ALL until go-live." How would you respond?
Executive Answer
I would explain that while I understand the business urgency, granting SAP_ALL would
introduce significant audit and security risks and could invalidate Segregation of Duties
controls.
Instead, I would propose:
1. Identify the exact business processes causing delays.
2. Create temporary, time-bound project roles with only the necessary access.
3. Route the request through SAP GRC ARM for approval.
4. Perform an SoD analysis using ARA.
5. If emergency access is required, use Firefighter IDs (EAM) with appropriate approvals
and log reviews.
6. Remove temporary access immediately after go-live.
This approach balances operational needs with governance and ensures compliance
without exposing the organization to unnecessary risk.
Senior Interview Insight
At Lead and Architect levels, interviewers assess not only your technical knowledge but
also your judgment. They want to hear solutions that balance business enablement with
security and compliance. Avoid answers that focus solely on SAP transactions; always
explain the governance rationale behind your decisions.
