

AP S/4HANA SECURITY 
Chapter 1 – S/4HANA Security Architecture & Transformation Strategy 
 
Q111. What are the major security differences between ECC and S/4HANA? 
Interviewer's Objective 
This is probably the most common S/4HANA interview question. 
The interviewer wants to know whether you understand that S/4HANA is not simply 
ECC running on HANA. 
 
Executive Answer 
Although S/4HANA retains the SAP authorization concept based on roles and 
authorization objects, the security architecture has evolved significantly. 
Key differences include: 
ECC S/4HANA 
SAP GUI-centric SAP Fiori-first experience 
Transaction-based access App-based access 
Limited analytics Embedded Analytics 
Customer/Vendor Master Business Partner (BP) 
Classic UI SAP Fiori Launchpad 
Limited OData usage Extensive OData services 
Separate frontend Integrated user experience 
 
Biggest Change 
In ECC 
Security focused primarily on 
• Transaction Codes  
• Authorization Objects  

• GUI  
In S/4HANA 
Security must also protect 
• Fiori Apps  
• OData Services  
• CDS Views  
• Launchpad  
• Business Partner  
• Embedded Analytics  
 
Q112. Explain Greenfield, Brownfield and Selective Data Transition. 
A favorite S/4HANA interview question. 
 
Greenfield 
Completely new implementation. 
Advantages 
• Fresh RBAC Design  
• Clean Security Model  
• Modern Fiori Roles  
• No legacy authorization issues  
Security Focus 
• Business Personas  
• New Role Design  
• Fiori Security  
• SoD Design  
 
Brownfield 
System Conversion. 

Existing ECC converted. 
Advantages 
• Faster implementation  
Challenges 
• Legacy roles  
• Obsolete transactions  
• Historical SoD issues  
Security Focus 
• Role cleanup  
• Transaction replacement  
• SU24 review  
• Rule Set update  
 
Selective Data Transition 
Hybrid approach. 
Only selected business processes or data are migrated. 
Security Focus 
• Mixed role landscape  
• Data segregation  
• Access consistency  
• Dual-system governance  
 
Interview Tip 
Always explain the security implications, not just the implementation type.

Q115. Explain Business Partner Security. 
 
Executive Answer 
S/4HANA replaces the traditional Customer and Vendor master with the Business 
Partner (BP) concept. 
Security must therefore control: 
• BP Creation  
• BP Maintenance  
• BP Display  
• BP Relationships  
• BP Roles  
This introduces new authorization considerations compared to ECC. 
 
Interview Tip 
When discussing S/4HANA Security, always mention Business Partner because 
interviewers expect candidates to understand this major simplification. 
 
Q116. How do you approach Security during an S/4HANA Migration? 
 
Executive Answer 
I follow a structured methodology. 
Step 1 
Inventory existing roles. 

 
Step 2 
Remove obsolete transactions. 
 
Step 3 
Review SU24 proposals. 
 
Step 4 
Conduct business workshops. 
 
Step 5 
Identify business personas. 
 
Step 6 
Design enterprise RBAC. 
 
Step 7 
Design Fiori security. 
 
Step 8 
Run SAP GRC ARA. 
 
Step 9 
Perform SIT/UAT. 
 
Step 10 
Support cutover and hypercare. 
 

Key Principle 
Never perform a "lift and shift" of ECC roles into S/4HANA. 
Use the transformation as an opportunity to redesign and simplify the security 
model. 
 
Q117. Explain Security Testing in an S/4HANA Project. 
 
Executive Answer 
Security testing should occur throughout the implementation lifecycle. 
Unit Testing 
Validate individual roles and authorizations. 
 
System Integration Testing (SIT) 
Verify end-to-end business processes across modules. 
 
User Acceptance Testing (UAT) 
Confirm that business users can complete their daily activities with the assigned 
roles. 
 
Regression Testing 
Ensure new changes do not break existing access. 
 
Security Validation 
Confirm: 
• Authorization objects  
• Fiori access  
• OData services  
• Organizational values  
• SoD compliance

Q120. Real Interview Scenario 
Interviewer 
"The business asks you to migrate all ECC roles directly into S/4HANA to save time. 
What would you recommend?" 
 
Executive Answer 
I would advise against a direct migration without analysis. 
Although it may reduce short-term effort, it also carries forward legacy 
authorization issues, obsolete transactions, and existing SoD conflicts. 
Instead, I would recommend: 
1. Inventory and analyze current roles.  
2. Remove obsolete transactions.  
3. Validate business processes for the future-state S/4HANA environment.  
4. Define business personas and an enterprise RBAC model.  
5. Redesign roles to support Fiori applications, Business Partner, and 
embedded analytics.  
6. Validate with SAP GRC ARA.  
7. Test thoroughly before production.  
This approach requires more effort initially but produces a cleaner, more 
maintainable, and audit-ready security model that supports long-term business 
objectives. 
 
Architect's Insight 
One of the strongest messages you can give in an interview is: 
"An S/4HANA migration is not a technical upgrade; it is a business transformation. 
Security should therefore be redesigned to align with future-state business 
processes rather than simply copied from ECC." 

That statement reflects the mindset expected from a senior consultant or architect 
leading transformation programs.
