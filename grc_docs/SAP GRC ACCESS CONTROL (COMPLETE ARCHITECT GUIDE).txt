Q1. What is SAP GRC? Why do organizations
implement it?
Interviewer's Objective
The interviewer wants to understand whether you see GRC as more than just an SoD

Many candidates answer:

Executive Answer
SAP Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) is an enterprise governance platform that
enables organizations to manage user access, identify and mitigate access risks, enforce
Segregation of Duties (SoD), support regulatory compliance, and automate access
governance across SAP landscapes.
CATEGORY 4 – SAP GRC ACCESS CONTROL
(COMPLETE ARCHITECT GUIDE)
"GRC is used for SoD."

The objective of SAP GRC is to ensure that users receive the correct access at the right
time while reducing operational, financial, and compliance risks.
SAP GRC supports the entire access lifecycle—from requesting access to approving it,
provisioning it, monitoring its usage, and periodically reviewing it.
It acts as the governance layer above SAP Security rather than replacing SAP Security
itself.
Business Benefits
Organizations implement SAP GRC to:
Reduce fraud
Prevent unauthorized access
Enforce Segregation of Duties
Improve audit readiness
Automate user provisioning
Centralize access governance
Reduce manual approval effort
Improve compliance with SOX, ITGC, ISO 27001, GDPR, and internal policies
Support enterprise-wide digital transformation

Q5. Explain Segregation of Duties (SoD).
This is probably the single most important GRC interview question.
Executive Answer
Segregation of Duties is a preventive internal control designed to ensure that one
individual cannot perform conflicting business activities that could result in fraud, financial
loss, or unauthorized transactions.
The objective is to separate critical business functions among multiple users.
Example
A user should not have both:
Vendor Creation
AND
Vendor Payment
because the same individual could create a fraudulent vendor and immediately issue
payment.
Common SoD Examples
Function 1Function 2
Create VendorPay Vendor

Function 1Function 2
Create Purchase OrderApprove Purchase Order
Maintain CustomerReceive Customer Payments
Create Journal EntryApprove Journal Entry
Create UserAssign Security Roles
Why SoD Matters
Without SoD:
Fraud risk increases.
Financial reporting becomes unreliable.
Audit findings increase.
Regulatory compliance is weakened.

Preventive Controls
Prevent issues before they occur.
Examples:
SAP GRC ARA
Approval workflows
Least Privilege
RBAC
SoD validation during role design
Detective Controls
Identify issues after they occur.
Examples:
Audit reports
Firefighter log review
Periodic access review

Security monitoring
SIEM alerts
Best Practice
A mature organization uses both preventive and detective controls together.
Q7. What are Critical Access and Critical Actions?
Critical Access
Refers to highly sensitive authorizations.
Examples:
SAP_ALL
User Administration
Table Maintenance
Debugging
Production Configuration
Critical Actions
Specific high-risk business transactions.
Examples:
Vendor Creation
Bank Master Maintenance
Payment Execution
Role Maintenance
User Creation
Difference
Critical Access focuses on sensitive permissions, while Critical Actions focus on high-
risk business activities.
Q8. What is Risk Analysis in SAP GRC?

Executive Answer
Risk Analysis evaluates user or role authorizations against predefined risk rules to identify:
SoD conflicts
Critical Access
Critical Permissions
Sensitive Transactions
Risk analysis can be performed for:
Users
Roles
Profiles
HR Objects
It can also be executed before provisioning (preventive) or periodically after provisioning
(detective).
Q9. What is a Risk Rule Set?
Executive Answer
A Risk Rule Set is the repository of business rules used by SAP GRC to identify access
risks.
It defines:
Conflicting functions
Sensitive transactions
Authorization object combinations
Risk severity
Business impact
Example:
Risk Name:
"Vendor Creation vs Payment"
Function 1:
Vendor Master Maintenance
Function 2:
Payment Processing

If a user has access to both functions, SAP GRC flags an SoD violation.

Real Interview Scenario
Interviewer
"A business user insists they need both Vendor Creation and Vendor Payment access
because they work in a small plant. What would you do?"
Executive Answer
I would first validate the business requirement with the process owner to understand why
both responsibilities are needed. If segregation is genuinely not feasible due to staffing
constraints, I would avoid granting unrestricted access by default.
Next, I would assess the SoD conflict in SAP GRC ARA and evaluate compensating
controls such as enhanced management approval, independent review of payments, or
increased monitoring. If the risk is accepted by the business, it should be formally
documented with an approved mitigation control and reviewed periodically.
The objective is not simply to eliminate every SoD conflict but to manage business risk in a
controlled and auditable manner. Decisions should balance operational requirements with
governance and compliance.
