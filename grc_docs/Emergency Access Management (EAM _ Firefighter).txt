

CATEGORY 4 – SAP GRC ACCESS CONTROL 
Chapter 4 – Emergency Access Management (EAM / Firefighter) 
 
Q38. What is Emergency Access Management (EAM)? 
Interviewer's Objective 
The interviewer wants to understand whether you know EAM as a governance 
process rather than simply "Firefighter IDs." 
 
Executive Answer 
Emergency Access Management (EAM), commonly referred to as Firefighter, is a 
SAP GRC Access Control module that provides temporary, controlled, and fully 
auditable privileged access to production systems during emergencies. 
Instead of permanently assigning highly privileged roles such as SAP_ALL, 
organizations use Firefighter IDs to grant temporary elevated access for activities 
like production issue resolution, emergency configuration changes, or business-
critical support. 
Every Firefighter session is: 
• Approved  
• Time-bound  
• Logged  
• Reviewed  
• Audited  
The primary objective is to balance operational continuity with governance and 
compliance. 
 
Business Benefits 
EAM enables organizations to: 
• Avoid permanent privileged access  
• Maintain audit compliance  
• Resolve production incidents quickly  

• Ensure accountability for privileged activities  
• Meet SOX and ITGC control requirements  
 
Q39. Why do organizations implement Firefighter IDs? 
 
Executive Answer 
Organizations implement Firefighter IDs because business-critical situations 
occasionally require elevated access that users do not normally possess. 
Examples include: 
• Production system outages  
• Month-end financial close  
• Go-live support  
• Hypercare activities  
• Emergency configuration fixes  
• Security incident response  
Granting permanent administrator access for these scenarios would significantly 
increase risk. 
Firefighter provides controlled, temporary access instead.

Q41. What is the difference between Firefighter ID and Firefighter Role? 
This is a very common interview question. 
 
Firefighter ID 
A dedicated emergency user account. 
Example: 
FF_ADMIN01 
The Firefighter ID is shared only through the approved EAM process and is intended 
for temporary privileged access. 

 
Firefighter Role 
A standard SAP role containing elevated authorizations. 
The role is assigned temporarily to a user through SAP GRC without requiring a 
separate emergency user ID. 
 
Comparison 
Firefighter ID Firefighter Role 
Dedicated emergency user Temporary assignment of privileged role 
Separate login identity Uses existing user ID 
More common in older implementations More common in modern environments 
 
Q42. Who is a Firefighter Owner? 
 
Executive Answer 
The Firefighter Owner is responsible for: 
• Managing Firefighter assignments  
• Ensuring access validity  
• Assigning Controllers  
• Maintaining ownership records  
• Ensuring emergency access remains appropriate  
The Owner is responsible for governance. 
The Owner does not approve every emergency action. 
 
Q43. Who is a Firefighter Controller? 
One of the most frequently asked audit questions. 
 
Executive Answer 

The Firefighter Controller independently reviews all activities performed during 
Firefighter sessions. 
Responsibilities include: 
• Reviewing activity logs  
• Validating business justification  
• Confirming only approved actions were performed  
• Identifying unauthorized activities  
• Escalating suspicious behavior  
The Controller acts as an independent governance reviewer. 
 
Important Principle 
The Controller should not be the same person who performed the emergency work. 
Independent review is a key audit control.

Q45. What information is captured in Firefighter Logs? 
 
Executive Answer 
The logs capture: 
• User name  
• Firefighter ID  
• Login time  

• Logout time  
• Executed transaction codes  
• Executed reports  
• Table changes  
• Configuration changes  
• Time stamps  
• System information  
These logs provide a complete audit trail of emergency activities. 
 
Q46. What should a Controller review? 
 
Executive Answer 
The Controller should verify: 
• Was the emergency justified?  
• Were only approved transactions executed?  
• Were any unauthorized changes made?  
• Were sensitive tables modified?  
• Was debugging used?  
• Were transports created?  
• Was the emergency access duration appropriate?  
If anomalies are found, they should be investigated and documented. 
 
Q47. Common Audit Findings in EAM 
A favorite interview topic. 
 
Executive Answer 
Common findings include: 
Permanent Firefighter Assignments 

Emergency access should never become permanent. 
 
Missing Controller Reviews 
Logs exist but are never reviewed. 
 
Shared Credentials 
Firefighter IDs shared informally. 
 
Missing Business Justification 
No documented reason for emergency access. 
 
Excessive Authorizations 
Firefighter contains unnecessary privileges. 
 
Expired Assignments 
Temporary assignments remain active. 
 
Lack of Log Retention 
Logs deleted too early or not retained according to policy. 
 
Q48. How would you design an Emergency Access Strategy? 
Architect-level question. 
 
Executive Answer 
My strategy consists of: 
Step 1 
Identify business-critical scenarios. 
 

Step 2 
Define Firefighter IDs or Firefighter Roles. 
 
Step 3 
Assign Owners. 
 
Step 4 
Assign independent Controllers. 
 
Step 5 
Configure approval workflows. 
 
Step 6 
Enable activity logging. 
 
Step 7 
Define review frequency. 
 
Step 8 
Periodically review unused Firefighter IDs. 
 
Step 9 
Conduct audit validation. 
 
Step 10 
Report KPIs to governance committees. 
 
Q49. Firefighter During S/4HANA Go-Live 

Very common in transformation interviews. 
 
Executive Answer 
During go-live, emergency access demand increases significantly due to 
production support. 
I typically recommend: 
• Predefined Firefighter IDs for technical teams  
• Time-bound assignments  
• Dedicated Controllers  
• Daily log reviews during hypercare  
• Separate IDs for Basis, Security, and Functional teams  
• Removal of temporary assignments immediately after stabilization  
This ensures operational agility while maintaining governance. 
 
Q50. Interview Scenario 
Interviewer 
"An auditor discovers that a Firefighter ID has not been reviewed for six months. 
What would you do?" 
 
Executive Answer 
I would treat this as a governance failure. 
My approach would be: 
1. Confirm the period affected and identify all sessions without review.  
2. Review the Firefighter logs retrospectively to determine what activities were 
performed and whether any unauthorized actions occurred.  
3. Discuss the issue with the Firefighter Owner and Controller to understand 
why the review process failed.  
4. Document the finding, perform a risk assessment, and, if necessary, raise it 
through the organization's audit or compliance process.  

5. Implement corrective actions such as automated reminders, escalation for 
overdue reviews, periodic governance dashboards, and management 
oversight.  
6. Verify that future Firefighter reviews are completed within the organization's 
defined SLA.  
This addresses the immediate risk while strengthening the control framework to 
prevent recurrence. 
 
Advanced Interview Questions (Rapid Fire) 
Q51. Can Firefighter IDs be shared? 
Answer: They should only be used through the controlled EAM process. 
Organizations should avoid unmanaged credential sharing. Modern 
implementations increasingly favor Firefighter Roles or controlled assignment 
mechanisms that provide better accountability. 
 
Q52. Who owns Firefighter IDs? 
Answer: The designated Firefighter Owner. 
 
Q53. Who reviews Firefighter logs? 
Answer: The Firefighter Controller. 
 
Q54. Is Firefighter a preventive or detective control? 
Answer: Primarily detective, because activities are reviewed after execution. 
However, approval workflows and time-bound assignments introduce preventive 
elements. 
 
Q55. Should Firefighter IDs have SAP_ALL? 
Answer: Not by default. They should contain only the privileged authorizations 
required for the specific emergency scenarios they are intended to support. 
Granting SAP_ALL unnecessarily increases risk and audit exposure. 
 

Manufacturing Scenario 
Interviewer 
"It's 2:00 AM. A production line has stopped because a configuration issue in 
S/4HANA is preventing goods movements. The only consultant available requires 
elevated access. What would you do?" 
Executive Answer 
I would activate the organization's emergency access process rather than granting 
permanent privileged access. 
1. Confirm the business impact and urgency with the operations lead.  
2. Assign an approved Firefighter ID or Firefighter Role for a limited duration.  
3. Ensure the required approvals are obtained according to the emergency 
procedure.  
4. Resolve the production issue using only the necessary authorizations.  
5. End the emergency session immediately after the fix.  
6. Ensure the Controller reviews the session logs and documents the business 
justification and actions taken.  
This restores operations quickly while preserving governance and auditability.
